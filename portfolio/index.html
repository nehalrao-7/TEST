00 The premise

They say you can only connect the dots looking backwards. I've spent a decade collecting them on purpose — gears, grids, code, customers — so when the next hard problem shows up, the line is already drawn.

Words light up as you read. Pacing is a design material too.

01 How I think

01 / Sketch

Start with a feeling, not a feature.

Before CAD, before specs, I sketch what it should feel like to use. Loose lines are cheap — they let ten bad ideas die so one good one can live.

02 / Engineer

Then make it honest.

Every line gets a number: dimensions, tolerances, materials, cost. If a sketch can't survive a spreadsheet, it doesn't deserve a factory.

03 / Explode

Take it apart before the world does.

I design the disassembly as carefully as the product — how it's built, serviced, repaired. Fewer parts, fewer screws, fewer ways to fail.

04 / Ship

Then make it real.

The finish is where trust is earned — the click of a bezel, the weight in a hand. Engineering gets it working. Craft gets it loved.

Object — Lumen, a lamp + speaker concept · drawn live in your browser

Every part is generated in code: cylinders, tori, a lathe. No models were imported.

02 Working style

Three habits.
Try them yourself.

Habit 01 — Vision

See the shape before it exists.

Every project starts as scattered dots: a complaint from a customer, a constraint from a supplier, a weird idea at 2 a.m. My job is to see the line through them before anyone else can.

Connect the dots, 1 → 13

There it is. That's the job — seeing the idea before it's drawn.

Habit 02 — Precision

Tight isn't better. Right is better.

Every tolerance is a trade between cost, feel and how it'll be built. Too tight and it won't assemble. Too loose and it rattles by Tuesday. I care about the hundredths of a millimetre so users never have to.

Drag to set the clearance

+0.22 mm Loose — it'll rattle by Tuesday

Habit 03 — Iteration

Prototype ugly. Finish beautiful.

The first version should be fast and a little embarrassing — cardboard, hot glue, a note that says "button??". The last one should feel quiet and inevitable. Everything in between is listening.
